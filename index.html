How insurance-based financing works

Fund the policy. Keep your capital working.

Permanent life insurance is often the right tool for estate, succession or buy-sell planning, but paying premiums from cash ties up capital that could stay in your practice or business. A Thornquay facility pays the premiums instead.

You

Hold the policy

You or your corporation own a permanent policy chosen with your insurance advisor.

Thornquay

Funds the premiums

A credit facility pays the premiums. The policy's value is assigned as collateral, with other security if needed.

Exit

Repaid as planned

The facility is repaid on a schedule agreed at the start, from cash flow, a liquidity event or the policy itself.

Typical situations

The kinds of problems we are asked to solve.

Incorporated professional

Estate planning without draining the corporation

A physician with retained earnings in a professional corporation wants permanent coverage for the next generation, but needs the capital for a clinic expansion.

Approach: premiums funded by a facility secured on the policy, repaid on a planned schedule.

Business owners

Funding a buy-sell agreement

Two partners in a private company need insurance to fund a buy-sell agreement, without diverting cash from operations during a growth phase.

Approach: a facility for each policy, structured with the company's lawyer and accountant.

Existing structure

Refinancing a legacy arrangement

A family holds a leveraged insurance arrangement whose lender terms no longer suit them, and wants a second opinion and clearer terms.

Approach: a full review of the current terms and, where it makes sense, a refinancing.

Illustrative scenarios, not client cases.
